Design spec · Listed feeds · 2026-10-03 · proposed

Listed feeds

Some RSS feeds Jon wants filtered, and some he wants to see in full. A Listed feed skips the Edition's judgment, and its new stories wait behind one door on Today, so Today keeps its shape. Decision: DECISIONS §33 (proposed).

Today every RSS Stream lands in the Tail, where most stories are declined. That's right for a firehose, and wrong for NYT Travel or Dining, where a declined story is exactly the reminder Jon asked for. The Gate 4 ratification left this as a per-Stream choice to make when it came up. Now it has.

Screened or ListedA per-Stream choice for RSS. Screened is today's behavior and stays the default. Listed is never judged and never calls a model.
One Feeds doorCounts per feed and the newest headline, beside the offer doors. No feed rows on Today, and no change to "to process".
The Feeds sheetOne section per feed, newest first. Tapping a headline opens the NYT app or Safari, where the subscription is signed in.
Three days, then goneA listed story shows for three days after it's published. The NYT keeps the article, and Later is the way to hold one.
01 · Today

A door, not a section

About 15 stories a day across five feeds would take over a column. The door gives the count, where they came from, and the newest headline, then gets out of the way. With nothing new, it disappears.

Today · the Feeds door under Offers

"New" is narrow. Not opened, not dismissed, and published in the last three days. Open a story and it stops counting.

Outside the queue. "14 to process" and the Process tab stay email and Tail only. The Feeds sheet is where these are read, the same move §30 made for offers.

The index line counts it. "Feeds 16" sits after Offers, so the shape of the morning still reads from one line.

02 · The Feeds sheet

Every new story, grouped by feed

A large system sheet over Today, like quick look. Close it and Today is where it was. Each feed is a section in Following order, newest first inside it. A row is the headline, the byline, and one line of the feed's own description. Cockpit writes none of it.

Feeds · one row opened, one mid-swipe
ActionWhat happens
Tap a headlineThe system opens the article: the NYT app if it's installed, otherwise Safari (as Daily links do, §29). The row dims and is marked Opened.
Swipe: DismissThe story leaves the sheet, with Undo. Dismiss is the Edition's word for resolving a non-email piece (§15).
Swipe or menu: LaterInto Later, under the existing rules. Nothing enters Later on its own.
Dismiss allClears every listed story at once, with one Undo. The door disappears.
Do nothingThree days after it's published, the story leaves without a trace.
03 · Choosing

Asked once, when you add the feed

Add Stream asks after it finds the feed. Screen stays the default, so nothing about an existing Stream changes until Jon chooses. The same two options sit in the Stream's Following detail.

Add Stream · NYT Travel, choosing Listed

Essential turns off. Essential promises a story can't age away. A Listed story does age away, on purpose, so the switch is disabled while a Stream is Listed.

The first poll is handled by the window. A new feed arrives with about 20 stories of backfill. Only the last three days show.

Switching is forward-looking. It takes effect at the next poll and composition. Dismissed stories stay dismissed, and nothing is re-judged.

04 · For Jon

To settle on this page

  1. Three days. Long enough to catch a weekend's Travel piece on Monday, short enough that Book Review's 5 a day doesn't pile up. Two or seven are both defensible.
  2. The newest-headline teaser. It's what makes the door a reminder rather than a number. Without it the door is one line shorter.
  3. The name. "Feeds" is plain and accurate. "Just published" is the alternative.
  4. Which feeds to list. It's per-Stream, so Movies, the noisiest of the five, with interviews and news mixed in, could stay Screened while Travel and Dining are Listed.
  5. Schema. One synced table of opened and dismissed stories, plus a new handling case. Both need Jon's yes before slicing, and both devices update together.

Not included: push notifications when a feed publishes, keyword filters inside a feed, thumbnails, and reading NYT articles inside Cockpit. Feed counts come from the five NYT feeds as measured on 2026-10-03. Headlines, descriptions and bylines are invented to show density, because this repository is public.
